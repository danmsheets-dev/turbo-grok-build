## What this is, in 60 seconds

You have an open-source multi-agent AI system on GitHub. You sell HubSpot AI automation and Generative Engine Optimization to B2B companies.

**Those audiences don't overlap**, so this campaign does not market the app. It uses the app as *proof* — the verifiable evidence that your firm builds real AI systems — and converts that credibility into requests for a **free AI Search Readiness Audit**, which is the natural on-ramp to your $3,500 GEO sprint.

Three post types, rotating:
- **A — Proof:** a real capability from the codebase, translated into what it means for a business.
- **B — Lesson:** something that broke, why, and the rule it teaches about running AI in a company. *(Highest engagement.)*
- **C — Offer:** AEO/GEO education with a direct CTA to the audit.

## Three decisions only you can make

### 1. The anonymity conflict 🔴 Blocking

Your About page says your partners keep no public profiles, deliberately, because of their day jobs. The repo is published under a **personal** GitHub account with a personal email in the commit history. Promoting it from the RevenueDrivenAI brand account links the two.

**Recommended:** create a `revenuedrivenai` GitHub organization and transfer or mirror the repo. ~30 minutes, removes the conflict, and a company-owned org is a *stronger* entity signal for AEO than a personal account. Details in `01-STRATEGY.md` §6.

### 2. AEO or GEO? 🟠 Do this before writing the landing page

Your site says **GEO** everywhere. You briefed the CTA as **AEO**. Both terms are in live buyer use; splitting them dilutes both.

**Recommended:** call the product **"AI Search Readiness Audit"** and subtitle it *"Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) / Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)"*. Plain-English name, both acronyms indexed, consistent with the GEO service page you already have. All copy in this plan uses that convention.

### 3. How honest do you want to be about the blog? 🟡

Your blog is currently four pages of automated AI news roundups. For a firm selling AI search visibility, that's the most available objection against you. The campaign fixes it by replacing it with answer assets. You can do that quietly, or you can make it a Week 4 post — *"we audited ourselves and our own blog was the worst thing on the site"* — which is on-brand and would perform well.

**Recommended:** fix it quietly in Week 1, then tell the story on Day 26 once it's already fixed.

## Running the campaign each week

```
Sunday      60 min   Schedule the week's posts (copy is already written)
Mon–Fri     30 min   Post, then reply to every comment within 90 minutes
Tue + Thu   90 min   Publish 2 answer assets
Wednesday   45 min   Reddit — 3 substantive answers, no link drops
Thursday    45 min   Dev.to article (weeks 2–4)
Friday      30 min   Metrics review, adjust next week's hooks
```

**~6.5 hrs/week**, plus ~90 min per audit delivered.

**The single highest-leverage habit:** reply to every comment within 90 minutes of posting. On LinkedIn that is the biggest reach lever you control, and it's free.

## How to use the copy

Every post is paste-ready, but treat the **hook line** as yours to swap. After the first week you'll see what your specific audience responds to — the Friday review exists to catch that. The substance should stay; the opener can flex.

**Placeholders:** anything in `[BRACKETS]` is a real number you must fill in from your own data. **Never publish a bracketed placeholder**, and never substitute an invented number for one.

**LinkedIn link rule:** the link goes in the first comment, never the post body. Each post ships with its first comment written.
